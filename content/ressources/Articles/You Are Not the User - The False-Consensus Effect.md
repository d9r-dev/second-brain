*By Raluca Budiu, October 22, 2017*

## Summary

The article discusses the **false-consensus effect**, a psychological bias where individuals assume others think and behave like they do. In user experience (UX) and software development, this leads professionals to design systems that reflect their own preferences and experiences rather than those of actual users.

Raluca Budiu emphasizes that even designers, developers, and UX researchers are vulnerable to this bias, often mistaking their behaviors as representative of their users. The article urges practitioners to overcome this cognitive trap by **testing designs with real users**, rather than relying on personal judgment or feedback from colleagues.

The piece provides insight into how **availability bias**, **generalization**, and other cognitive biases affect our assumptions and design decisions. It underscores the need for humility, self-awareness, and methodological rigor in UX work.

## Key Quotes

> “You are not the user.”

> “Assuming that you are your user is a fallacy that is ingrained in the human mind.”

> “The false-consensus effect refers to people’s tendency to assume that others share their beliefs and will behave similarly in a given context.”

> “We, designers, developers, and UX researchers assume that people who will use our interfaces are like us.”

> “Test. With real users (not your colleagues). Know who your users are and how they respond to your designs by watching them use these designs.”

> “Acknowledge your vulnerability and establish checks. Don’t validate; instead investigate.”

## Key Takeaways

- **False-consensus effect**: Overestimating how much others agree with us.
- **Availability bias**: Drawing conclusions based on what’s familiar or readily available.
- **Testing is essential**: Personal experience is not a substitute for real-user feedback.
- **Cognitive biases affect experts too**: Even UX researchers need safeguards against their own assumptions.
